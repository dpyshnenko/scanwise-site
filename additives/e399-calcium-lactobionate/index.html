What it is

A calcium salt of lactobionic acid used as a firming agent and calcium supplement.

How it is used in food

Calcium lactobionate is a very soluble, mild-tasting calcium salt used to fortify foods and to firm cut fruit and vegetables by cross-linking pectin in cell walls. It also functions as a sequestrant and antioxidant stabiliser, and unlike some calcium salts it does not impart bitterness or grittiness, so it works well in clear beverages and dairy products.

Commonly found in
  • Desserts
  • Calcium supplements

Health and safety

Completely safe, though because it is derived from lactose, it may not be suitable for strict vegans.

JECFA evaluated calcium lactobionate and allocated it an ADI 'not specified' — its highest safety category — for use as a firming agent and calcium source. Lactobionic acid is poorly absorbed and largely fermented by colonic bacteria, so very high intakes can have a laxative effect, as with other poorly absorbed sugar acids and polyols. Because it is manufactured by oxidising lactose from milk, it is not suitable for strict vegans and, although the protein content is negligible, people with severe milk-protein allergy may prefer to avoid it.

Worth knowing

Lactobionate's real fame is in transplant medicine: it is the key osmotic component of University of Wisconsin solution, the standard preservation fluid that allowed livers and pancreases to be stored for many hours before implantation and transformed abdominal organ transplantation in the 1980s.
