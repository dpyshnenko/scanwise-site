What it is

Sunset Yellow FCF (E110), known in the US as Yellow 6, is a synthetic orange azo dye. It provides a bright yellow-orange color to foods.

How it is used in food

Provides a vibrant synthetic yellow-orange color.

Commonly found in
  • Orange sodas (like Fanta or Irn-Bru in original formulations)
  • Cheeses (to make cheddar look bright orange)
  • Snack foods and crisps
  • Jellies and candies

Health and safety

Linked to hyperactivity in children.

Like Tartrazine (E102), Sunset Yellow is a member of the "Southampton Six" and is strongly linked to childhood hyperactivity and attention deficit. It also has the potential to cause allergic reactions.

Hyperactivity in children

children · evidence: probable

The 2007 Southampton trial reported that mixtures including this colour increased hyperactive behaviour in 3-, 8- and 9-year-olds. EFSA did not accept the trial as establishing a causal link for any individual colour, but the EU legislated the warning label anyway: "May have an adverse effect on activity and attention in children."

Southampton Study on Food Colours and Hyperactivity

Allergic reactions

aspirin-sensitive individuals · evidence: established

Can cause hives, rhinitis, and asthma, especially in individuals with aspirin intolerance.

EFSA re-evaluation of Sunset Yellow FCF (E 110)

EFSA sharply cut its acceptable daily intake in 2009 before partially relaxing it after new data.

Alternatives to this additive

  • E160b: Annatto — Natural plant extract that gives cheddar cheese its classic orange color without hyperactivity risks.
  • E160c: Paprika extract — Natural red/orange color.

How it is made

Derived from petroleum and coal tar through chemical synthesis.

  1. Synthesis — Synthesized from petroleum derivatives.

Worth knowing

It is a petroleum-derived azo dye from the "Southampton six".

Conclusion

E110 is heavily scrutinized. Due to the mandatory EU warning label, many major brands have quietly replaced it with natural alternatives like paprika extract or annatto.
