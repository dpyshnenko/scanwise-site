What it is

Butylated hydroxyanisole (E320), commonly known as BHA, is a synthetic antioxidant. It is extremely effective at preventing fats and oils from oxidizing (going rancid), extending the shelf life of greasy foods by months or even years.

How it is used in food

Synthetic antioxidant.

Commonly found in
  • Chewing gum
  • Dehydrated potato flakes
  • Cereals (often sprayed onto the packaging liner)
  • Cosmetics and lip balms

Health and safety

IARC classifies it "possibly carcinogenic" (2B) from rodent forestomach tumors.

BHA is one of the most controversial food additives still in use. The International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) classifies it as Group 2B (possibly carcinogenic to humans).

Suspected carcinogen

general population · evidence: suspected

Classified by the IARC as a Group 2B possible human carcinogen based on animal studies showing it causes forestomach tumors in rats. It is also listed under California Prop 65 as a known carcinogen.

IARC Monograph on BHA

Endocrine disruption

general population · evidence: suspected

Some studies suggest BHA may act as an endocrine disruptor, interfering with hormone function.

It is on the EU’s endocrine-disruptor screening list.

Alternatives to this additive

  • E306: Tocopherols — Natural Vitamin E extracts that provide similar fat-protecting benefits without the cancer risks.
  • E392: Rosemary extract — A natural, plant-based antioxidant.

How it is made

It is a petrochemical derivative synthesized by reacting 4-methoxyphenol with isobutylene.

  1. Synthesis — Derived from petrochemicals via the reaction of 4-methoxyphenol and isobutylene.

Worth knowing

Vitamin E does the exact same job safely.

Conclusion

Despite the IARC classification and a California Prop 65 warning, regulatory agencies like the FDA and EFSA still permit its use in very small amounts, arguing that the exposure levels in human diets are far below the doses that cause tumors in rats. Nonetheless, due to consumer pressure, many companies are voluntarily phasing it out in favor of natural antioxidants like Vitamin E (E306).
