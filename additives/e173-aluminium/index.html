What it is

E173 is elemental aluminium used purely for aesthetic purposes to give a shiny, metallic silver finish to the surface of foods. It is almost exclusively used in confectionery and cake decorating.

How it is used in food

Used for silver decoration.

Commonly found in
  • Silver dragées (the hard silver balls on cakes)
  • Metallic cake decorations and icing
  • Sugar-coated chocolates and pastries

Health and safety

Contributes to total dietary aluminium, which accumulates in the body.

Aluminium is neurotoxic and affects bone health if it accumulates in the body over time. Because of this, health authorities have established a strict Tolerable Weekly Intake (TWI), and the use of E173 is heavily legally restricted to prevent widespread dietary exposure.

Neurotoxicity and bone toxicity

general population · evidence: established

Excessive and prolonged exposure to aluminium leads to its accumulation in the body, which has been shown to cause neurotoxicity (damage to the nervous system) and impair bone formation.

Safety of aluminium from dietary intake

Dietary aluminium accumulation is a recognized concern.

How it is made

Elemental aluminium is milled into an extremely fine metallic powder or formulated into thin flakes.

  1. Milling — Pure elemental aluminium is ground into microscopic particles or flakes to create a silver pigment.

Worth knowing

EFSA keeps it on a tight weekly limit.

Conclusion

E173 is entirely cosmetic. While occasional consumption on a decorative cake or pastry is unlikely to exceed the weekly safety thresholds, it is an unnecessary source of a known neurotoxin and is easily avoided.
