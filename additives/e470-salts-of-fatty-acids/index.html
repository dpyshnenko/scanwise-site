What it is

A generic categorization for sodium, potassium, calcium, and magnesium salts of fatty acids (like E470a and E470b).

How it is used in food

The sodium, potassium, calcium (E 470a) and magnesium (E 470b) salts of fatty acids act as emulsifiers, anticaking agents and release agents — keeping powdered drink mixes, spice blends and tablets free-flowing, stopping products sticking to moulds and belts, and stabilising fat-in-water systems.

Commonly found in
  • Baked goods
  • Confectionery

Health and safety

Metabolized safely like normal dietary fats.

EFSA's 2018 re-evaluation of E 470a and E 470b found no safety concern at reported use levels and set no numerical ADI: the fatty acid part is metabolised like any dietary fat and the mineral part joins the body's normal calcium, magnesium, sodium or potassium pool. The fatty acids can come from plant or animal fat, which is a labelling rather than a safety question.

Worth knowing

Chemically these are soaps: calcium stearate and sodium stearate are the same class of compound as a bar of hand soap. At the fractions of a percent used in food they simply behave as lubricants, and magnesium stearate is the most widely used tablet lubricant in the world.

Conclusion

Safe, but vegans should verify if the fats were plant-sourced.
