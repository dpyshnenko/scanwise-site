E474 · Emulsifiers

Sucroglycerides

No known risk

A safe emulsifier. Check with the manufacturer if avoiding animal products.

What it is

An emulsifier made by reacting sucrose (sugar) with natural fats.

How it is used in food

An efficient emulsifier at low doses, it disperses fat evenly in ice cream, coffee whiteners, dairy analogues and instant powders, improving whipping performance and preventing fat separation and feathering in hot drinks.

Commonly found in
  • Ice cream
  • Sauces
  • Confectionery

Health and safety

Safe for human consumption; breaks down into sugar and fats during digestion.

EFSA's re-evaluation of sucrose esters of fatty acids and sucroglycerides found no safety concern at reported use levels and maintained a group acceptable daily intake covering both. Digestion separates them into sucrose and fatty acids, both handled by normal metabolism; the fat source may be animal or vegetable, which matters for vegan diets rather than safety.

Worth knowing

It is made by reacting sucrose directly with an edible fat or oil, so the product is not a single compound but a mixture of sucrose fatty acid esters plus mono- and diglycerides — in effect E 473 and E 471 produced together in one step, which is why the two additives share an intake limit.

Conclusion

A safe emulsifier. Check with the manufacturer if avoiding animal products.
