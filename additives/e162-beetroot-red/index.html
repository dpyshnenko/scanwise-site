What it is

Beetroot Red (E162) is a natural red to pink food colouring. Its active pigment is betanin, which is exactly the same pigment that gives beetroot its deep red colour.

How it is used in food

Natural red alternative.

Commonly found in
  • Strawberry and raspberry flavored yoghurts
  • Ice creams and sorbets
  • Pink candies and desserts
  • Plant-based meats (to mimic a raw, red appearance)

Health and safety

None known; main "side effect" is red urine (beeturia).

E162 is exceptionally safe. It is literally just concentrated beetroot juice. The only known "side effect" is beeturia—where consuming large amounts can harmlessly turn your urine pink or red.

Harmless pink urine (Beeturia)

general population · evidence: established

Consuming high amounts can cause urine to turn pink or red. This is harmless and passes quickly.

EFSA re-evaluation of Beetroot Red (E 162)

How it is made

It is simply extracted from red beet juice and concentrated.

  1. Extraction — Beetroots are pressed for their juice, and the betanin pigment is concentrated.

Worth knowing

It is betanin, squeezed directly from beetroots.

Conclusion

As a completely natural, plant-based colour, Beetroot Red is the ultimate clean-label alternative to controversial synthetic red dyes like Allura Red (E129) and animal-based dyes like Carmine (E120).
