E282 · Preservatives

Calcium propionate

Limited risk preservative

Bread preservative; debated gut effects at high doses.

Category
Preservatives
Vegan
Yes
Vegetarian
Yes

What it is

A propionate salt used specifically as a highly effective anti-mold agent in baked goods.

How it is used in food

The bakery standard — it holds mould off sliced bread while leaving the yeast to work.

Commonly found in
  • Sliced bread
  • Tortillas
  • Pastries
  • Pita bread

Health and safety

The same picture as the rest of the propionate group: a normal product of fibre fermentation in your own gut, with open questions about metabolic effects at high intakes.

Regulatory bodies worldwide consider E282 safe. Propionates occur naturally in some foods (like Swiss cheese) and are even produced by human gut bacteria.

Possible behavioral effects

children · evidence: disputed

Some limited studies and anecdotal reports suggest E282 may cause irritability and restlessness in children, though this is not officially recognized by EFSA or the FDA.

Long-standing authorisation for bread and baked goods.

Alternatives to this additive

  • E200: Sorbic acid — Often used instead, though it can interfere with yeast rising.

How it is made

Produced by neutralizing propionic acid (which occurs naturally in Swiss cheese) with a mineral base.

  1. Neutralization — Propionic acid is reacted with calcium hydroxide to form the salt.

Worth knowing

Calcium propionate is the single most-used bread preservative in the world, which is why the group gets studied more than its obscurity would suggest.

Conclusion

Despite official safety approvals, there is a live, controversial debate. Some small-scale studies and persistent consumer reports suggest that regular consumption of E282 may cause irritability, restlessness, and behavioral changes, particularly in children. More research is needed, but many parents avoid it out of caution.
