What it is

E407a is a natural thickener and gelling agent extracted from red seaweeds. It is essentially a less refined version of carrageenan (E407), containing a higher percentage of cellulose (plant fibre).

How it is used in food

A less-purified carrageenan straight from farmed eucheuma seaweed.

Commonly found in
  • Plant-based milks (almond, soy) to prevent separation
  • Injected into processed meats (ham, poultry) to retain water
  • Dairy desserts and ice creams

Health and safety

Shares the carrageenan degradation debate point for point.

Because E407a is essentially unrefined carrageenan, it shares the exact same safety controversies. While regulatory bodies like EFSA deem it safe, some independent studies suggest that degraded carrageenan (poligeenan) can trigger intestinal inflammation, though this is heavily debated.

Potential intestinal inflammation

general population · evidence: suspected

Like carrageenan, some studies suggest that in acidic environments (like the stomach), it may degrade into smaller inflammatory molecules, though human clinical evidence is inconclusive.

How it is made

Red seaweed (Eucheuma cottonii or Eucheuma spinosum) is washed, soaked in a hot alkaline solution, dried, and milled into a powder.

  1. Alkaline Treatment — Seaweed is treated with hot alkali to modify its structure.
  2. Drying and Milling — Unlike E407, it is not filtered to remove cellulose, making it cheaper to produce.

Worth knowing

It keeps some of its natural cellulose.

Conclusion

E407a is widely used as a cheaper alternative to E407 in processed meats and dairy alternatives. If you actively avoid carrageenan due to digestive concerns, you should also avoid E407a.
