What it is

Brilliant Blue FCF (E133), also known in the US as Blue 1, is a synthetic blue dye. It provides a bright, intense blue colour and is often mixed with yellow dyes (like E102) to create green.

How it is used in food

Provides a brilliant blue color, often paired with tartrazine to make greens.

Commonly found in
  • Blue raspberry flavored candies and drinks
  • Ice creams and popsicles
  • Canned peas (mixed with yellow to restore green colour lost in canning)
  • Mouthwashes and toothpaste

Health and safety

None major at food doses.

Unlike many synthetic red and yellow dyes, Brilliant Blue FCF is not part of the "Southampton Six" and is not strongly linked to hyperactivity. The body absorbs very little of it (less than 5%), meaning it mostly passes straight through the digestive system.

Harmless green/blue stools

general population · evidence: established

Because E133 is poorly absorbed by the intestines, consuming large amounts (like drinking a brightly colored blue sports drink or eating blue frosting) will turn feces green or blue. This is entirely harmless.

EFSA re-evaluation of Brilliant Blue FCF (E 133)

Allergic reactions

asthmatics · evidence: suspected

In very rare cases, E133 can cause allergic reactions, particularly in individuals with pre-existing asthma.

Alternatives to this additive

  • Spirulina extract — A completely natural blue colour derived from blue-green algae, rapidly replacing E133 in clean-label candies.

How it is made

Like most synthetic dyes, it is derived from petroleum byproducts through complex chemical synthesis.

  1. Synthesis — Synthesized from coal tar or petroleum derivatives via the condensation of benzaldehyde with aniline derivatives.

Worth knowing

It is a triarylmethane blue.

Conclusion

E133 is generally considered safe. However, because it is entirely synthetic and derived from petroleum, clean-label advocates prefer natural blue alternatives.
