What it is

A calcium salt used as a thickener, gelling agent, and dietary calcium/phosphorus supplement.

How it is used in food

Calcium glycerophosphate supplies both calcium and phosphorus in a single soluble, well-tolerated salt, making it useful for fortifying beverages, dairy alternatives and supplements without the chalky mouthfeel of calcium carbonate. It also acts as a buffer and stabiliser, and its alkalinity means it helps neutralise acids — a property exploited in oral-care products and some acidic drinks.

Commonly found in
  • Dental products
  • Dietary supplements

Health and safety

Safe and serves as a bioavailable source of minerals.

JECFA and national authorities have evaluated calcium glycerophosphate as a nutrient source and found no toxicological concern at use levels; the EU permits it as a source of calcium and phosphorus in food supplements and fortified foods. EFSA's assessments of glycerophosphate salts concluded the glycerophosphate moiety is hydrolysed and metabolised normally, so the safety question reduces to total calcium and phosphorus intake. Clinical interest centres on enamel remineralisation, where trials of glycerophosphate-containing dentifrices show modest benefit, and on its use in low-phosphate states.

Worth knowing

It is added to some chewing gums and toothpastes as an anti-caries agent: by raising the concentration of calcium and phosphate ions in saliva and buffering plaque acid, it supports remineralisation of early enamel lesions. The salt was historically marketed as a general 'nerve tonic' in the early twentieth century, a claim with no evidential basis.
