What it is

Carotenes (E160a), particularly Beta-carotene, are natural orange-yellow pigments. They are found abundantly in nature, famously giving carrots, pumpkins, and sweet potatoes their bright orange colour.

How it is used in food

Acts as provitamin A.

Commonly found in
  • Margarine and butter (to give a golden yellow look)
  • Orange juices and multivitamin drinks
  • Yoghurts and dairy desserts
  • Clean-label candies

Health and safety

None known; eating excess just turns skin harmlessly orange.

Carotenes are exceptionally safe and actually beneficial to health. The human body naturally converts beta-carotene into Vitamin A (retinol) exactly as needed. Excess amounts are simply stored safely in fat or excreted.

Harmless orange skin (Carotenosis)

general population · evidence: established

Eating massive amounts of carotenes can cause the skin to temporarily turn yellow or orange. This is completely harmless and reverses when intake drops.

EFSA re-evaluation of carotenes

How it is made

E160a can be extracted naturally from plants (like carrots and palm oil), fermented by specific fungi (Blakeslea trispora), or synthesized chemically to be identical to nature.

  1. Extraction or Synthesis — Extracted with solvents from carrots/algae, or synthesized via fermentation/chemical processes.

Worth knowing

The same pigment found in carrots and pumpkins.

Conclusion

E160a is the gold-standard clean-label coloring. It is widely used to replace controversial artificial dyes like Tartrazine (E102) and Sunset Yellow (E110).
