Caustic sulphite caramel

What it is

Caustic sulphite caramel (Class II) is caramel colour made with sulphite but no ammonia.

How it is used in food

Provides caramel color for spirits like whisky and brandy.

Commonly found in
  • Whisky and brandy
  • Vinegars
  • Sauces

Health and safety

Negligible residual sulphite.

Residual sulphite levels are considered safely negligible.

Conclusion

You do not need to steer clear of caustic sulphite caramel unless you are extremely sensitive to sulphite compounds. Produced without ammonium, this spirit and sauce colourant leaves only negligible residual sulphites that EFSA considers safely within its 300 mg per kg body weight daily limit. For the vast majority of people, its presence in whisky or vinegar is of minor concern.
