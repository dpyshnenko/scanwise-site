What it is

E155, or Brown HT (Chocolate Brown HT), is a synthetic brown azo dye used primarily to replace cocoa or caramel colouring in highly processed foods. It is entirely artificial and has no nutritional value.

How it is used in food

Used to color chocolate cakes.

Commonly found in
  • Chocolate-flavoured milk and milkshakes
  • Cheap chocolate cakes and biscuits
  • Jams, jellies, and fruit preserves

Health and safety

Potential health concerns that led to bans.

Unlike the famous "Southampton Six" colours, Brown HT does not require a mandatory hyperactivity warning in the EU. However, it is an azo dye and shares the same structural concerns regarding allergic reactions and asthma triggers.

Hypersensitivity reactions

aspirin-sensitive individuals · evidence: suspected

As an azo dye, it may provoke allergic reactions, including hives (urticaria) and asthma, particularly in individuals who are sensitive to aspirin.

EFSA re-evaluation of Brown HT

Banned in the US, Canada, and much of Scandinavia while permitted in the EU within limits.

Alternatives to this additive

  • E150a: Plain caramel — A safer, non-azo brown colouring derived from heated sugars.
  • E150c: Ammonia caramel — Commonly used to provide deep brown colours in baked goods and drinks.

How it is made

It is manufactured chemically from petroleum-derived aromatic compounds (specifically, diazo coupling of hydroxynaphthalenesulfonic acids).

  1. Chemical Synthesis — Synthesised through complex chemical reactions involving petroleum derivatives (azo coupling).
  2. Purification — The dye is purified to remove unwanted by-products before being dried into a powder.

Worth knowing

It is a synthetic azo brown.

Conclusion

Brown HT is a purely cosmetic dye with a poor global regulatory standing. Given that natural alternatives like cocoa powder and plain caramel exist, its presence usually indicates a highly processed, lower-quality product.
