What it is

Ponceau 4R is a synthetic azo dye giving a strawberry red, one of the "Southampton six".

How it is used in food

Provides a strawberry-red color.

Commonly found in
  • Confectionery
  • Dessert toppings
  • Soft drinks
  • Processed fish products

Health and safety

Linked to hyperactivity in children.

E124 is one of the "Southampton Six" artificial colours associated with increased hyperactivity and reduced attention in children — the finding that led the EU to require a warning label on any food containing them.

Hyperactivity in children

children · evidence: probable

Linked to increased hyperactivity and reduced attention in children in the 2007 Southampton trial, which tested it in a mixture with sodium benzoate. EFSA did not accept a causal link for the individual colour, but the EU legislated a mandatory warning label anyway.

Southampton study (Lancet 2007)

Aspirin cross-reactivity

sensitive individuals · evidence: suspected

Case reports describe urticaria and asthma-like reactions to azo colours in sensitive people, sometimes alongside aspirin sensitivity. EFSA reviewed the intolerance literature in 2009 and found it too limited to quantify.

EFSA evaluation of E124

EFSA cut its acceptable intake fourfold in 2009.

Worth knowing

It is an azo dye from the "Southampton six", banned in the US.

Conclusion

In the EU, any product containing E124 must carry a prominent warning label about its effects on children's attention. Due to this stigma, it has been largely removed from mainstream products but still lingers in cheap imports and discount sweets. It is entirely banned in the United States.
