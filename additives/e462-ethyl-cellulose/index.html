Ethyl cellulose

No known risk

A widely used and safe modified plant fiber.

What it is

A chemically modified cellulose derivative used as a binder, film former, and thickener.

How it is used in food

Ethyl cellulose forms tough, flexible, water-insoluble films, which makes it the coating and encapsulation material of choice for flavours, sweeteners, vitamins and supplement tablets, giving moisture protection and controlled release. It also serves as a binder and a filler where a non-swelling cellulose is needed.

Commonly found in
  • Food supplements
  • Encapsulated flavors
  • Bakery products

Health and safety

Safe and completely inert; functions as dietary fiber in the body.

EFSA re-evaluated the modified celluloses (E 461, E 462, E 463, E 465, E 466, E 468, E 469) in 2018 and concluded there is no safety concern at reported use levels, retaining a group ADI 'not specified'. The substances are not absorbed and pass through the gut largely intact.

Worth knowing

It is the odd one out among the food cellulose ethers: while carboxymethyl, methyl and hydroxypropyl celluloses dissolve in water to thicken, ethylation makes cellulose water-repellent, so E 462 is used to keep water out rather than to hold it in.

Conclusion

A widely used and safe modified plant fiber.
