What it is

Tartrazine (E102), widely known in the US as Yellow 5, is a synthetic lemon-yellow azo dye. It is one of the cheapest and most common food colorings in the world.

How it is used in food

Provides a bright synthetic yellow color.

Commonly found in
  • Sodas (like Mountain Dew) and energy drinks
  • Macaroni and cheese boxes
  • Chips (like Doritos) and popcorn
  • Candies and ice lollies

Health and safety

Linked to increased hyperactivity in children.

Tartrazine is one of the most heavily scrutinized artificial dyes. It belongs to the "Southampton Six" group of dyes linked to increased hyperactivity in children. Furthermore, of all the azo dyes, tartrazine appears to cause the most allergic and intolerance reactions.

Hyperactivity in children

children · evidence: probable

The 2007 Southampton trial reported that mixtures including this colour increased hyperactive behaviour in 3-, 8- and 9-year-olds. EFSA did not accept the trial as establishing a causal link for any individual colour, but the EU legislated the warning label anyway: "May have an adverse effect on activity and attention in children."

Southampton Study on Food Colours and Hyperactivity

Allergic reactions

aspirin-sensitive individuals · evidence: established

Tartrazine can trigger asthma, hives, and nasal congestion, particularly in individuals who are sensitive to aspirin.

EFSA re-evaluation of Tartrazine (E 102)

The 2007 Lancet trial linked it to hyperactivity; EU products must carry a warning label.

Alternatives to this additive

  • E160a: Carotenes — Natural, safe orange-yellow pigment.
  • E100: Curcumin — Derived from turmeric, provides a natural yellow color.

How it is made

Like most azo dyes, it is derived from petroleum byproducts through a complex chemical synthesis.

  1. Synthesis — Derived from petroleum, synthesized via the azo coupling reaction.

Worth knowing

It is one of the "Southampton six" colors.

Conclusion

E102 is generally avoided by health-conscious consumers. Because of the mandatory hyperactivity warning label in Europe, many EU manufacturers have replaced it with natural alternatives like beta-carotene (E160a) or turmeric (E100).
