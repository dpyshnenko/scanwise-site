What it is

E150 on its own is not one additive — it is the family name for four caramel colours made by four different processes.

How it is used in food

One of the cheapest and most stable ways to give a food a brown colour.

Commonly found in
  • Soft drinks
  • Soy sauce and gravy
  • Dark beers and spirits
  • Baked goods

Health and safety

Which caramel you are eating matters, and a bare "E150" does not say. The two ammonia classes, E150c and E150d, carry traces of 4-methylimidazole (4-MEI), which caused tumours in high-dose mouse studies and is capped by regulation. The plain and sulphite classes, E150a and E150b, do not involve ammonia and do not carry 4-MEI.

EFSA set a group ADI of 300 mg/kg body weight per day covering all four caramel classes, with a separate, tighter limit on 4-MEI in the ammonia classes.
