What it is

Sulphur dioxide (E220) is a toxic gas at room temperature, but dissolved in water it forms sulfurous acid, an incredibly potent antimicrobial and antioxidant preservative. It has been used since the Roman era to sterilize winemaking equipment and preserve food.

How it is used in food

Stops browning and microbial growth; the ancient wine preservative.

Commonly found in
  • Wine and cider (prevents spoilage and oxidation)
  • Dried fruits like apricots (keeps them orange instead of turning brown)
  • Lemon juice concentrates
  • Dehydrated potatoes

Health and safety

A genuine allergen (affects 3–10% of asthmatics) and destroys vitamin B1 (thiamine).

For the vast majority of the population, dietary sulphites are processed harmlessly by the liver into sulfates and excreted. However, for a specific subset of people (particularly those with severe asthma), sulphites can trigger terrifying and potentially fatal allergic reactions.

Asthma exacerbation and anaphylaxis

asthmatics and sulphite-sensitive individuals · evidence: established

Sulphites can trigger severe respiratory distress (bronchospasm) in sensitive individuals, primarily those who already suffer from severe asthma. In extreme cases, it can cause anaphylactic shock.

EFSA re-evaluation of sulfur dioxide and sulfites

Destruction of Vitamin B1 (Thiamine)

general population · evidence: established

Sulphur dioxide chemically destroys Vitamin B1. For this reason, it is banned from being used in foods that are considered major sources of thiamine, such as fresh meats.

EFSA re-evaluation of sulfur dioxide and sulfites

Alternatives to this additive

  • E300: Ascorbic acid — Can replace some of the antioxidant functions of sulphites, but lacks the antimicrobial punch.

How it is made

Industrially, it is produced by burning elemental sulfur or sulfur-containing ores in the presence of oxygen.

  1. Combustion — Elemental sulfur is burned in air to produce sulfur dioxide gas.
  2. Dissolution — The gas is either compressed into a liquid for direct injection into food processes (like winemaking) or dissolved in water to form a solution.

Worth knowing

Romans burned sulphur candles in barrels to preserve wine.

Conclusion

E220 is a powerful, historic preservative that remains practically irreplaceable in winemaking and fruit drying. Because of its severe allergenic potential, any product containing more than 10 ppm of sulphites must carry a bold allergen warning.
