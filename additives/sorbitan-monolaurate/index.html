Sorbitan monolaurate

What it is

A synthetic emulsifier used primarily to manage how fats behave during heating and cooling.

How it is used in food

In chocolate, it prevents "fat bloom" — that unappealing white powdery coating that forms when chocolate is poorly stored.

Commonly found in
  • Chocolate
  • Baking yeast
  • Cake mixes
  • Margarine

Health and safety

Safe at typical dietary levels, with EFSA establishing clear Acceptable Daily Intakes (ADIs) based on extensive safety data.

Evaluated by EFSA and confirmed safe for use in food at authorized levels.

Worth knowing

Sorbitan monostearate (E491) is also known as "Span 60" in industrial chemistry applications.

Conclusion

This synthetic emulsifier presents a bounded risk and only becomes an issue if consumed in excessive amounts. While EFSA has established an Acceptable Daily Intake based on safety data, the small quantities used to keep fat bloom from forming in chocolate or to stabilise cake mixes are well within safe limits for a standard diet.
