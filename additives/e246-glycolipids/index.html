What it is

Glycolipids (E246) are natural emulsifiers and preservatives used in the food industry. They consist of a carbohydrate attached to a lipid.

How it is used in food

E246 glycolipids are long-chain glycolipids produced by fermentation of the fungus Dacryopinax spathularia, authorised as a preservative rather than a texturiser. They are effective against yeasts, moulds and some bacteria in beverages, are active at low levels and at the acidic pH of soft drinks, and give producers a fermentation-derived alternative to benzoates and sorbates. Their amphiphilic structure does also give them surface activity, so they can help stabilise dispersions.

Commonly found in
  • Beverages
  • Sauces
  • Emulsions

Health and safety

Generally considered safe with no major health concerns reported.

EFSA's 2019 scientific opinion on glycolipids as a food additive found no safety concern at the proposed uses and levels in beverages, and the substance was subsequently added to the EU authorised list as E 246; it also holds GRAS status in the United States for beverage use. Because it is a recent and narrowly permitted addition, it appears in far fewer products than its E-number position among the older preservatives might suggest.

Worth knowing

The producing organism is an edible jelly fungus eaten in parts of Asia, and the same compounds are what protect the fungus itself from microbial competition — a rare case of a preservative borrowed directly from another organism's own chemical defences.
