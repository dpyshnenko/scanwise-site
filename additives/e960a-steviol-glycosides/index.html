What it is

Steviol glycosides (E960a) are the sweet chemical compounds extracted from the leaves of the Stevia rebaudiana plant. They are up to 300 times sweeter than sugar and contain zero calories.

How it is used in food

Commonly found in
  • Diet sodas and iced teas
  • Yoghurts and low-sugar dairy desserts
  • Tabletop sweeteners (often blended with erythritol)

Health and safety

Unlike artificial sweeteners, Stevia is extracted from a plant, giving it a strong "clean label" appeal. Extensive toxicology studies have shown it is safe, non-toxic, and does not spike blood sugar.

E960a is highly regarded as a safe, natural alternative to sugar. Its only real drawback is culinary: highly purified stevia extracts can trigger bitter receptors on the tongue, leaving a distinctive metallic or licorice-like aftertaste.

How it is made

The stevia leaves are steeped in hot water (like tea), and the sweet compounds are purified out of the liquid using alcohol and ion-exchange resins.

  1. Extraction — Stevia leaves are crushed and steeped in hot water.
  2. Purification — The extract is filtered and crystallized to isolate the pure steviol glycosides (like Rebaudioside A).

Conclusion

E960a is highly regarded as a safe, natural alternative to sugar. Its only real drawback is culinary: highly purified stevia extracts can trigger bitter receptors on the tongue, leaving a distinctive metallic or licorice-like aftertaste.
