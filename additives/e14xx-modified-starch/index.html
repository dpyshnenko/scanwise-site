Modified Starch

What it is

A general classification for starches that have been chemically or physically altered to improve their stability under heat, acid, or freezing.

How it is used in food

Modified starches thicken, stabilise and bind water far more reliably than native starch, holding sauces, soups, dressings and dairy desserts smooth through heating, freezing, acidic conditions and mechanical shear. They let manufacturers cut fat while keeping a creamy mouthfeel, prevent syneresis (weeping) in chilled and frozen ready meals, and are gluten-free, so they are widely used in coeliac-safe baking.

Commonly found in
  • Sauces
  • Soups
  • Frozen meals

Health and safety

Modified starches are generally recognized as safe and are metabolized similarly to natural starch.

EFSA's ANS Panel re-evaluated the whole group of modified starches in 2017 and concluded there was no safety concern at reported use levels, setting no numerical ADI — the digestive tract breaks most of them down to glucose like ordinary starch, while resistant fractions ferment in the colon much as dietary fibre does.

Worth knowing

The name causes constant confusion: "modified" here means physically or chemically treated starch (heat, acid, enzymes, esterification or cross-linking), and has nothing to do with genetic modification. The E14xx family covers roughly E1404 through E1452, each number a different treatment — E1414 for acetylated cross-linked starch, E1442 for the freeze-thaw workhorse hydroxypropyl distarch phosphate.

Conclusion

A broad, safe category of heavily processed plant starches used for texture.
