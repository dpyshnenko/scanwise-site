What it is

E285, commonly known as Borax, is the sodium salt of boric acid (E284). Like its parent compound, its use as a food additive is strictly limited to preserving genuine sturgeon caviar due to the toxicity of boron accumulation.

How it is used in food

Preserves sturgeon caviar.

Commonly found in
  • Genuine sturgeon caviar

Health and safety

Restricted heavily because boron accumulates in the body.

Boron compounds are toxic to the reproductive system if consumed in high doses. While EFSA has established a safe Tolerable Daily Intake, the primary strategy to protect the public from boron accumulation is to ban it in all foods except a luxury item that is eaten in tiny, infrequent quantities.

Reproductive toxicity

general population · evidence: established

Animal studies show that excessive exposure to boron compounds can impair fertility and fetal development. Its strict restriction in food prevents this risk in humans.

How it is made

It is naturally occurring and mined from evaporite deposits, then purified.

  1. Mining and Purification — Borate minerals are mined, dissolved in water, filtered, and recrystallised to produce pure borax.

Worth knowing

Everywhere else it’s a cleaning-product ingredient, not a food one.

Conclusion

Unless you frequently consume real sturgeon caviar, you will never encounter E285 in your diet. Its approval exists merely to support a highly specific, traditional luxury food industry.
