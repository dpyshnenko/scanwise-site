Inosinic acid

What it is

An acid that strongly enhances umami flavors, especially when mixed with MSG.

How it is used in food

Inosinic acid (IMP) intensifies meaty, brothy flavors and works synergistically with glutamate, allowing reduced salt and MSG levels while keeping full savory taste.

Commonly found in
  • Snack seasonings
  • Instant meals

Health and safety

Like guanylate, it metabolizes into uric acid; gout sufferers should moderate intake.

JECFA evaluated inosinic acid and its salts and found no toxicological concerns at use levels, setting no numerical ADI. Commercial production is typically by microbial fermentation, though it can be derived from meat or fish.

Worth knowing

IMP is the compound that makes katsuobushi (dried bonito flakes) so savory — its accumulation in fish and meat after slaughter is part of why aged meat tastes richer.
