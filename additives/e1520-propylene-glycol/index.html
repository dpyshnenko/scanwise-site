What it is

Propylene glycol (E1520) is a synthetic, slightly syrupy liquid. It is primarily used as a carrier solvent for flavor extracts and colors, or as a humectant to keep baked goods moist.

How it is used in food

The solvent that carries most liquid food flavorings.

Commonly found in
  • Flavor extracts (like vanilla or artificial fruit flavors)
  • Food coloring
  • Soft baked goods and frosting

Health and safety

Its bad press mostly comes from also being used in e-cigarettes and antifreeze formulations — at food doses it’s uneventful.

EFSA’s limit is generous relative to food exposure.

Alternatives to this additive

  • E422: Glycerol — A naturally derived humectant that provides similar moisture-retaining properties.

How it is made

It is a petrochemical derivative, produced by the hydration of propylene oxide.

  1. Synthesis — Propylene oxide is hydrated with water under heat and pressure.

Worth knowing

Metabolized to lactic acid.

Conclusion

Propylene glycol is completely safe. The human body metabolizes it into lactic acid—the exact same chemical your muscles produce when you exercise. It is efficiently processed and excreted, leaving no toxic residue.
