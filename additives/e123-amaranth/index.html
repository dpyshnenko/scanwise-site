What it is

Amaranth (E123) is a dark red-to-purple synthetic azo dye. (Note: It has absolutely no relation to the natural amaranth grain or plant).

How it is used in food

Commonly found in
  • Aperitif wines and spirits
  • Fish roe (caviar substitutes)

Health and safety

E123 has a notoriously toxic reputation. In 1976, following Russian studies linking it to cancer, the US FDA completely banned it, famously sparking the "Red Dye Panic" of the 1970s.

Suspected carcinogen

general population · evidence: suspected

Linked to tumor formation in early rat studies, prompting its ban in the US, though later EFSA reviews found the evidence inconclusive.

Color Additives History (FD&C Red No. 2 delisted, January 1976)

While the EU (EFSA) concluded in 2010 that the cancer risk was unproven and maintained its approval, they drastically slashed its acceptable daily intake (ADI) to just 0.15 mg/kg. It is now heavily restricted and rarely used, typically only found in cheap caviar substitutes or niche alcoholic drinks.

How it is made

It is an azo dye derived from petrochemicals (coal tar).

  1. Synthesis — Produced chemically via the diazo coupling of naphthol derivatives.
