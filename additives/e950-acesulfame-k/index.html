What it is

Acesulfame Potassium (E950), commonly known as Ace-K, is an artificial zero-calorie sweetener. It is roughly 200 times sweeter than table sugar.

How it is used in food

200× sweeter than sugar, zero calories.

Commonly found in
  • Diet and zero-sugar sodas (like Coke Zero and Diet Pepsi)
  • Sugar-free chewing gum
  • Protein powders and sports supplements
  • Sugar-free desserts

Health and safety

Long-term epidemiology (including WHO’s 2023 advice against sweeteners for weight control) keeps the debate open.

Ace-K has a slightly bitter aftertaste, so it is almost never used alone. It is usually blended with Aspartame (E951) or Sucralose (E955) because they mask each other's flaws and create a more sugar-like taste. In the body, it is completely ignored—it passes through the digestive system unabsorbed and is excreted in urine unchanged within 24 hours.

Potential long-term metabolic effects

long-term consumers · evidence: suspected

The WHO advises against using artificial sweeteners like Ace-K for weight control, suggesting long-term use may actually increase the risk of type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular diseases, though the mechanism is not fully understood.

How it is made

It is a synthetic chemical produced by transforming acetoacetic acid derivatives into a complex potassium salt.

  1. Synthesis — Synthesized from acetoacetamide and reacted with sulfur trioxide to form a ring structure, which is then neutralized with potassium hydroxide.

Worth knowing

Not metabolized at all — it leaves your body unchanged within 24 hours.

Conclusion

While food safety authorities globally classify E950 as safe, long-term epidemiological studies have raised questions. In 2023, the WHO officially advised people not to use Ace-K and other artificial sweeteners for weight control, citing potential long-term risks of type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease.
