What it is

Citric acid (E330) is a weak organic acid that occurs naturally in citrus fruits like lemons and limes. Almost all commercial citric acid, however, is produced by fermenting sugars with the mould Aspergillus niger rather than extracted from fruit. In the food industry, it is used as a flavouring to provide a sour taste, an acidity regulator to maintain pH, and a preservative to prevent spoiling.

How it is used in food

Provides a tart, refreshing flavor while naturally preserving foods by lowering their pH.

Commonly found in
  • Soft drinks and carbonated beverages
  • Jams and fruit preserves
  • Sweets and sour candies
  • Canned fruits and vegetables
  • Ice cream and sorbets

Health and safety

None known.

Citric acid is one of the most thoroughly tested and universally accepted food additives. It is a natural intermediate in the human body’s metabolism (the citric acid cycle) and raises no safety concerns.

Erosion of tooth enamel

general population · evidence: established

Frequent consumption of highly acidic foods and beverages (like sour candies or sodas containing citric acid) can erode tooth enamel over time.

EFSA re-evaluation of citric acid (E 330)

Every cell in your body makes and burns citric acid continuously.

How it is made

While originally extracted directly from lemon juice, modern commercial citric acid is almost entirely produced through the fermentation of sugars (like molasses or corn steep liquor) by the mould Aspergillus niger.

  1. Preparation — A sugar source, such as corn syrup or molasses, is prepared and sterilized.
  2. Fermentation — The mould Aspergillus niger is added to the sugar solution and allowed to ferment for several days.
  3. Filtration — The mould is filtered out of the resulting liquid.
  4. Precipitation — Calcium hydroxide is added to form calcium citrate, which precipitates out of the solution.
  5. Recovery — Sulfuric acid is added to convert the calcium citrate back into pure citric acid, which is then crystallized.

Worth knowing

Although named after citrus fruits, 99% of commercial citric acid is produced by feeding sugar to a specific strain of mold.

Conclusion

E330 is an extremely safe, naturally occurring acid that is ubiquitous in both nature and modern food processing. It provides essential tartness and acts as a safe preservative.
