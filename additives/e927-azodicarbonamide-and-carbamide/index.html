What it is

E927 is the legacy umbrella code covering two unrelated compounds, since separated into E927a (azodicarbonamide, a flour treatment) and E927b (carbamide, also called urea, a chewing-gum ingredient).

How it is used in food

Azodicarbonamide bleaches and strengthens dough; carbamide buffers acid in sugar-free gum.

Commonly found in
  • None (Banned)

Health and safety

The two substances behind this code are not comparable. Azodicarbonamide is banned in the EU and Australia and permitted in the US; the concern is the semicarbazide and urethane formed when it bakes, plus occupational asthma in workers handling the raw powder. Carbamide is a normal product of your own protein metabolism and is a different question entirely.

Not authorised in the EU as a flour treatment. Read E927a and E927b separately; this code tells you only that one of them is present.

Worth knowing

Azodicarbonamide is the "yoga mat chemical" of the 2014 headlines — it really is used as a foaming agent in plastics, which is true but says little about the amounts used in flour.

Conclusion

A banned, highly toxic dough conditioner.
