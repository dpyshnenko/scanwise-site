What it is

E541 is a synthetic chemical compound used primarily as a raising agent (baking powder) in commercial baked goods. It releases carbon dioxide slowly during baking, giving cakes a light, fluffy texture.

How it is used in food

A slow-acting baking acid.

Commonly found in
  • Commercial baking powders
  • Sponge cakes and scones
  • Processed cheese (as an emulsifier)

Health and safety

Aluminium contributes to total dietary exposure, prompting stricter rules.

EFSA strictly regulates all aluminium-containing additives through a combined Tolerable Weekly Intake (TWI). Because aluminium accumulates in the body and is a known neurotoxin, E541 is restricted to specific baked goods (like commercial scones and sponges) to limit public exposure.

Neurotoxicity from accumulation

general population · evidence: established

Long-term dietary exposure to aluminium can lead to accumulation in tissues, which is linked to neurological damage and impaired bone formation.

EFSA re-evaluation of sodium aluminium phosphate

EFSA tightened aluminium additive rules in 2012.

Alternatives to this additive

How it is made

Manufactured chemically by reacting alumina (aluminium oxide), phosphoric acid, and sodium hydroxide.

  1. Chemical Synthesis — Produced industrially using aluminium, phosphoric acid, and sodium compounds.

Worth knowing

It has largely vanished from EU recipes.

Conclusion

While permitted in specific baked goods, E541 contributes to dietary aluminium intake. Health-conscious consumers often prefer products using standard baking powder (without aluminium).
