What it is

Sodium stearoyl-2-lactylate (E481), or SSL, is a highly effective emulsifier and dough conditioner. It strengthens gluten and traps gas, which is the secret to the incredibly soft, squishy texture of supermarket bread.

How it is used in food

Strengthens gluten films — the reason sliced sandwich bread springs back when squeezed.

Commonly found in
  • Sliced bread and burger buns
  • Tortillas
  • Pancake and waffle mixes

Health and safety

None known.

Digested into everyday fat and lactate.

How it is made

It is synthesized by reacting stearic acid (a fatty acid) with lactic acid, and neutralizing it with sodium.

  1. Esterification — Stearic acid and lactic acid are reacted under heat and pressure, then neutralized with sodium.

Worth knowing

A stearic-acid/lactic-acid hybrid.

Conclusion

While chemically harmless, it is a marker of highly processed bread. The primary concern is for vegans and those keeping Kosher/Halal, as the stearic acid used can be derived from beef tallow or pork fat unless explicitly certified plant-based.
