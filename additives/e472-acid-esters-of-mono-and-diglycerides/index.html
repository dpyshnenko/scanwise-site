E472 · Emulsifiers

Acid esters of mono- and diglycerides

What it is

A broad category (including E472a-f) of synthetic fats used widely in commercial baking.

How it is used in food

This family — acetic (a), lactic (b), citric (c), tartaric (d), diacetyltartaric or DATEM (e) and mixed (f) esters — gives bakers dough that traps gas and loaves that stay soft for days, and gives fat systems stable aeration in whipped toppings, margarines and coffee whiteners. Each subtype is tuned to a different job, from crumb softening to emulsion stability.

Commonly found in
  • Bread
  • Cakes
  • Desserts

Health and safety

Completely safe; breaks down into common dietary fats and acids.

EFSA re-evaluated the whole E 472a–f family in 2019 and concluded there was no safety concern at reported use levels, with no numerical ADI needed. Digestion splits them into glycerol, fatty acids and the respective organic acid — all normal metabolites. Whether the fatty acids are plant- or animal-derived is a dietary-preference issue, not a risk one.

Worth knowing

All of them are built by reacting ordinary mono- and diglycerides (E 471) with a common food acid, so the finished emulsifier is assembled from pieces already present in the diet. DATEM (E 472e) is the workhorse of industrial bread and by far the most used member of the group.

Conclusion

Safe for consumption. Often animal-derived unless certified vegan.
